# Second-harmonic generation

## 1. Definition and physical picture

Second-harmonic generation (SHG) is the coherent second-order response in which two fields at frequency $\omega$ generate a polarization and a current at $2\omega$. With the time dependence $e^{-i\omega t}$ used here,

$$
P^a(2\omega)
=\varepsilon_0\chi^{abc}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)
E^b(\omega)E^c(\omega),
$$

$$
J^a(2\omega)
=\sigma^{abc}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)
E^b(\omega)E^c(\omega).
$$

In the clean analytic theory, $J^a(2\omega)=-i2\omega P^a(2\omega)$. The implementation evaluates the susceptibility and conductivity with separate finite-width kernels, however, so neither numerical output is constructed from the other by a complex-frequency multiplication or division.

This release implements the independent-particle, electric-dipole, length-gauge response. The interband part describes coherent polarization generated by virtual and resonant transitions. The transport part contains two-band current terms and, at finite temperature, the Berry-curvature-dipole (BCD) and semiclassical/nonlinear-Drude Fermi-surface terms.

## 2. Core theoretical formula

Let

$$
\omega_{mn}=\frac{\varepsilon_m-\varepsilon_n}{\hbar},
\qquad
f_{nm}=f_n-f_m,
\qquad
\widetilde\omega=\omega+i\eta/\hbar,
$$

and define, for $n\ne m$,

$$
r^a_{nm}=i\langle u_{n\mathbf k}|\partial_{k_a}u_{m\mathbf k}\rangle,
\qquad
r^{a;b}_{nm}
=\partial_{k_b}r^a_{nm}
-i(\xi^b_{nn}-\xi^b_{mm})r^a_{nm},
$$

$$
v_n^a=\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_n}{\partial k_a},
\qquad
\Lambda^a_{nm}=v_n^a-v_m^a.
$$

The independent-particle quadratic response is decomposed as

$$
\bar\sigma_2^{abc}(\omega,\omega)
=-i2\omega\,\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{ter},2}^{abc}(\omega,\omega)
+\bar\sigma_{\mathrm{tra},2}^{abc}(\omega,\omega),
$$

$$
\chi^{abc}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)
=\frac{\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{ter},2}^{abc}}{\varepsilon_0}
+\frac{i\bar\sigma_{\mathrm{tra},2}^{abc}}{2\omega\varepsilon_0}.
$$

### Interband polarizability

In the corrected version of Eq. (22a) of Garcia-Goiricelaya, Krishna, and Ibañez-Azpiroz, the independent-particle interband polarizability is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{ter},2}^{\mathrm{KS},abc}
=&\frac{e^3}{2\hbar^2V}
\Bigg[
\sum_{\mathbf k mnl}
\frac{r^a_{nm}(r^b_{ml}r^c_{ln}+r^c_{ml}r^b_{ln})}
{\omega_{ln}-\omega_{ml}}
\left(
\frac{2f_{nm}}{\omega_{mn}-2\widetilde\omega}
+\frac{f_{ln}}{\omega_{ln}-\widetilde\omega}
+\frac{f_{ml}}{\omega_{ml}-\widetilde\omega}
\right)
\\
&+i\sum_{\mathbf k mn}\Bigg\{
f_{nm}\Bigg[
\frac{2r^a_{nm}(r^{b;c}_{mn}+r^{c;b}_{mn})}
{\omega_{mn}(\omega_{mn}-2\widetilde\omega)}
+\frac{r^{a;b}_{nm}r^c_{mn}+r^{a;c}_{nm}r^b_{mn}}
{\omega_{mn}(\omega_{mn}-\widetilde\omega)}
\\
&\qquad
+\frac{r^a_{nm}(r^b_{mn}\Lambda^c_{mn}+r^c_{mn}\Lambda^b_{mn})}
{\omega_{mn}^2}
\left(
\frac{1}{\omega_{mn}-\widetilde\omega}
-\frac{4}{\omega_{mn}-2\widetilde\omega}
\right)
\Bigg]
-\frac{r^a_{nm}(f_{nm;b}r^c_{mn}+f_{nm;c}r^b_{mn})}
{\omega_{mn}\omega}
\Bigg\}
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
$$

The correction published on 22 June 2023 fixes the three-band occupation combination to $+f_{ln}+f_{ml}$. The low-frequency factor in the last term is the ordinary $\omega$, not $\widetilde\omega$.

### Transport conductivity

The corresponding transport contribution, Eq. (22b), is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar\sigma_{\mathrm{tra},2}^{\mathrm{KS},abc}
=&\frac{e^3}{2\hbar^2V}
\Bigg\{
-\sum_{\mathbf k mn}f_{nm}
\Bigg[
\frac{r^{c;a}_{nm}r^b_{mn}+r^{b;a}_{nm}r^c_{mn}}
{\omega_{mn}-\widetilde\omega}
\\
&\qquad
+\frac{\Lambda^a_{nm}(r^b_{mn}r^c_{nm}+r^c_{mn}r^b_{nm})}
{2\omega(\omega_{mn}-\widetilde\omega)}
\Bigg]
\\
&+\sum_{\mathbf k n}\left[
\frac{i(f_{n;b}\epsilon_{dac}+f_{n;c}\epsilon_{dab})\Omega_n^d}{\omega}
-\frac{v_n^a f_{n;bc}}{\omega^2}
\right]
\Bigg\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last line contains the BCD and semiclassical one-band terms. At strictly zero temperature they are Fermi-surface distributions in a metal and do not generally vanish. Their omission by the legacy zero-temperature switch is therefore a numerical policy, not a general physical identity.

### Relation to the 1998 independent-particle formulation

Rashkeev, Lambrecht, and Segall (RLS 1998) start from the same length-gauge, independent-particle density-matrix framework, restricted to a filled-band system at zero temperature. They organize the total SHG susceptibility as a pure-interband part and a mixed interband--intraband part,

$$
\chi^{abc}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)
=\chi_e^{abc}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)
+\chi_i^{abc}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega).
$$

Garcia-Goiricelaya, Krishna, and Ibañez-Azpiroz (GKI 2023) instead separate the Kohn--Sham current into an interband polarization and a transport current. In susceptibility form their independent-particle response is

$$
\chi_{\mathrm{KS\text{-}IPA}}^{abc}
=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_0}
\left(
\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{ter},2}^{\mathrm{KS},abc}
+\frac{i}{2\omega}
\bar\sigma_{\mathrm{tra},2}^{\mathrm{KS},abc}
\right).
$$

After matching units, Brillouin-zone normalization, tensor-index order, and the causal-frequency prescription, the two organizations give the same total susceptibility for a zero-temperature insulator or clean semiconductor. The correspondence is most direct in the three-band sector. Expanding the RLS symmetrized product

$$
\{r_{ml}^b r_{ln}^c\}
=\frac{1}{2}
\left(r_{ml}^b r_{ln}^c+r_{ml}^c r_{ln}^b\right)
$$

makes RLS Eq. (2) identical term by term to the three-band part of corrected GKI Eq. (22a). In particular, both contain

$$
\frac{2f_{nm}}{\omega_{mn}-2\omega}
+\frac{f_{ln}}{\omega_{ln}-\omega}
+\frac{f_{ml}}{\omega_{ml}-\omega},
$$

with the GKI causal prescription written as $\omega\mapsto\widetilde\omega$.

The two-band correspondence is a relation between total responses rather than between names of printed terms. RLS Eq. (3) satisfies

$$
\left[\chi_i^{abc}\right]_{\mathrm{RLS\to SI}}
=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_0}
\left[
\bar\alpha_{\mathrm{ter},2}^{abc}\big|_{\mathrm{two\text{-}band}}
+\frac{i}{2\omega}
\bar\sigma_{\mathrm{tra},2}^{abc}\big|_{\mathrm{two\text{-}band}}
\right]_{\partial_{\mathbf k}f=0}.
$$

Thus the RLS labels `pure interband` and `mixed interband--intraband` are not one-to-one synonyms for the GKI labels `ter` and `tra`. GKI redistributes the generalized-derivative and velocity-difference structures between its polarization and transport formulas; they reproduce RLS only after the two-band pieces are combined as above.

For the filled-band limit used by RLS,

$$
f_{n;a}=0,
\qquad
f_{n;ab}=0.
$$

The occupation-derivative term in GKI Eq. (22a), together with the BCD and semiclassical terms in Eq. (22b), then vanishes. The two-band part of Eq. (22b) remains and contributes to the total susceptibility through $i\bar\sigma_{\mathrm{tra},2}/(2\omega)$.

RLS Eq. (4) further expands $r_{nm;a}^b$ into a band sum. GKI keeps the same gauge-covariant generalized derivative as a basic object, and this release evaluates it directly by Wannier interpolation. The expanded and direct forms agree in the complete-band continuum limit; finite-band interpolation and finite intermediate-state regularization define the corresponding numerical approximation.

## 3. Implemented decomposition and equivalent forms

### Hamiltonian-gauge Wannier vertices

The interpolation first constructs Hamiltonian-gauge matrix elements from the Wannier Hamiltonian and position operator. With $E_{mn}=\varepsilon_m-\varepsilon_n$ and $V^a_{nm}$ the Hamiltonian derivative in energy-length units,

$$
D^a_{nm}=\frac{V^a_{nm}}{E_{mn}},
\qquad
r^a_{nm}=A^a_{nm}+iD^a_{nm}.
$$

The endpoint dipole uses the bare gap, with $D^a_{nm}=0$ for $|E_{mn}|<10^{-7}$ eV. Intermediate contractions use

$$
\widetilde D^a_{nm}
=V^a_{nm}\frac{E_{mn}}{E_{mn}^2+\eta^2}.
$$

The generalized derivative is assembled from analytic Wannier interpolation of $A$, $H$, and their derivatives, rather than from a finite difference of eigenstate phases. When `eta_correction=true`, the finite-$\eta$ correction of Lihm, Eq. (19), is added so that regularization does not introduce a Bloch-sum phase-convention dependence.

### Two-band matrix combinations

The five recurring two-band combinations in the implemented energy convention are

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1^{abc}&=r^a_{nm}(r^{b;c}_{mn}+r^{c;b}_{mn}),\\
I_2^{abc}&=r^{a;b}_{nm}r^c_{mn}+r^{a;c}_{nm}r^b_{mn},\\
I_3^{abc}&=\frac{r^a_{nm}
(r^b_{mn}\Delta V^c_{mn}+r^c_{mn}\Delta V^b_{mn})}{E_{mn}},\\
I_4^{abc}&=r^{b;a}_{nm}r^c_{mn}+r^{c;a}_{nm}r^b_{mn},\\
I_5^{abc}&=\Delta V^a_{nm}
(r^b_{nm}r^c_{mn}+r^c_{nm}r^b_{mn}),
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
\Delta V^a_{pq}=V^a_{pp}-V^a_{qq},
\qquad
\Delta V^a_{mn}=-\Delta V^a_{nm}.
$$

The occupation-derivative term in the published interband formula is represented after integration by parts over a periodic Brillouin zone. Consequently, its implemented k-resolved density is not identical point by point to the unintegrated expression in Eq. (22a).

### Three-band combinations

For an intermediate band $p\ne n,m$, define the two contractions used by the implementation,

$$
T_{nmp}^{abc}
=r^a_{nm}
(\widetilde r^b_{mp}\widetilde r^c_{pn}
+\widetilde r^c_{mp}\widetilde r^b_{pn})
P_\eta(2E_p-E_n-E_m),
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{nmp}^{abc}
=&\widetilde r^a_{mp}
(\widetilde r^b_{pn}r^c_{nm}
+\widetilde r^c_{pn}r^b_{nm})
P_\eta(2E_n-E_m-E_p)
\\
&+\widetilde r^a_{pn}
(r^b_{nm}\widetilde r^c_{mp}
+r^c_{nm}\widetilde r^b_{mp})
P_\eta(2E_m-E_p-E_n),
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\widetilde r=A+i\widetilde D$. Every model band participates in the sum over $p$; the implementation does not introduce an additional SHG-specific intermediate-band cutoff.

### Finite-width frequency kernels

For an energy argument $x$ and positive width $g$,

$$
P_g(x)=\frac{x}{x^2+g^2},
\qquad
D_g(x)=P_g(x)+i\pi\delta_g(x).
$$

The supported resonance shapes are

$$
\delta_g^{\mathrm G}(x)
=\frac{e^{-(x/g)^2}}{\sqrt\pi g},
\qquad
\delta_g^{\mathrm L}(x)
=\frac{g}{\pi(x^2+g^2)}.
$$

At photon energy $\mathcal E=\hbar\omega$, the resonant kernels are

$$
d_1=D_g(E_{mn}-\mathcal E),
\qquad
d_2=D_g(E_{mn}-2\mathcal E),
\qquad
d_n=D_g(-E_{mn}-\mathcal E).
$$

The separately regularized low-frequency factors are

$$
L_\gamma(\mathcal E)=P_\gamma(\mathcal E),
\qquad
K_\gamma(\mathcal E)
=P_\gamma(\mathcal E)-i\delta_\gamma(\mathcal E).
$$

The absence of $\pi$ in the imaginary part of $K_\gamma$ is part of the WannierNLQG kernel convention. Resonance broadening, low-frequency broadening, and intermediate-state regularization therefore have distinct meanings and cannot be replaced by one complex photon energy.

### Corrected BCD and semiclassical terms

The Cartesian Berry-curvature two-form is

$$
F^n_{ac}=\epsilon_{acd}\Omega_n^d.
$$

The full Hamiltonian-gauge curvature used by this release is

$$
F^n_{ac}=\operatorname{Re}\left[
(\partial_aA_c-\partial_cA_a)_{nn}
+[A_c,D_a]_{nn}-[A_a,D_c]_{nn}
-i[D_a,D_c]_{nn}
\right].
$$

The two one-band tensors are

$$
B^n_{abc}
=f'_n(v_n^bF^n_{ac}+v_n^cF^n_{ab}),
$$

$$
S^n_{abc}
=f''_n v_n^av_n^bv_n^c
+f'_n v_n^a\varepsilon_{n,bc}.
$$

Thus

$$
B^n_{aaa}=0,
\qquad
B^n_{xyy}=2f'_n v_n^y\Omega_n^z.
$$

The BCD curvature includes the full model-band sum and the required Levi-Civita contraction. A band is excluded from the BCD contribution when either adjacent energy gap is within `degeneracy_threshold`; this is a numerical filter for the isolated-band expression and does not define a degenerate-subspace theory.

### Seven output groups

| Output group | Matrix content | Analytic origin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `interband_derivative` | $I_1$ and $I_2$ | Two-band generalized-derivative terms in Eq. (22a), including the integrated-by-parts low-frequency combination |
| `interband_velocity` | $I_3$ | Interband velocity-difference term in Eq. (22a) |
| `three_band` | Complete sums of $T_{nmp}$ and $U_{nmp}$ | Three-band term in corrected Eq. (22a) |
| `transport_derivative` | $I_4$ | Two-band generalized-derivative term in Eq. (22b) |
| `transport_velocity` | $I_5$ | Two-band velocity-difference term in Eq. (22b) |
| `berry_curvature_dipole` | $B^n_{abc}$ | First one-band term in Eq. (22b), with corrected curvature contraction |
| `semiclassical` | $S^n_{abc}$ | Second one-band term in Eq. (22b) |

These groups are algebraic parts of one response and are not asserted to be independently observable. Susceptibility and conductivity totals are each the sum of their own seven finite-width contributions.

## 4. Symbols, tensor indices, and normalization

The index $a$ is the output polarization or current direction, while $b,c$ are the two incident-field directions. Degenerate input frequencies imply intrinsic permutation symmetry,

$$
\chi^{abc}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega)
=\chi^{acb}(-2\omega;\omega,\omega),
$$

with the same relation for $\sigma^{abc}$. The release stores 18 independent components in axis-major order. For each $a=x,y,z$, the incident-field pairs are `xx`, `yy`, `zz`, `yz`, `xz`, and `xy`.

Uniform Brillouin-zone weights sum to one. With single-cell volume $\Omega_c$ and $N_k$ uniform points,

$$
\frac{1}{\Omega_cN_k}\sum_{\mathbf k}
=\frac{1}{\Omega_c}\sum_{\mathbf k}w_{\mathbf k}.
$$

The raw susceptibility kernel is multiplied by $e/(\varepsilon_0\Omega_c)\times10^{12}$ to give pm/V, and the raw conductivity kernel by $e^2/(\hbar\Omega_c)$ to give A/V$^2$. Each model band is one explicit electronic state; a spinor model receives no additional factor of two.

Integral output applies the normalized k-point weights. K-slice output contains the same normalized response density without a k-point weight. Its arithmetic mean reproduces the integral only when the slice points constitute a complete uniform Brillouin-zone mesh. A general plane is not a Brillouin-zone integral.

## 5. Key limits, symmetries, and applicability

- Electric-dipole SHG is odd under spatial inversion and vanishes in a centrosymmetric bulk crystal.
- Time-reversal symmetry alone does not forbid SHG.
- At `temperature <= 1e-7 K`, occupations are step functions and both one-band terms are skipped, following the legacy switch exactly. No positive surrogate temperature or extrapolation is used. This omits the distributional Fermi-surface response of a strictly zero-temperature metal.
- At higher temperature, occupations are Fermi--Dirac. The one-band occupation derivatives retain the reference truncated-Gaussian approximation within $5k_{\mathrm B}T$.
- A pair with a non-negligible occupation difference and $|E_{mn}|<10^{-12}$ eV is rejected. Near degeneracies also require care because isolated-band curvatures and Hessians are not invariant under arbitrary rotations of a degenerate subspace.
- Convergence with respect to the k mesh, Wannier window, model-band count, broadening, regularization, and degeneracy threshold is material dependent and is not implied by formula-level tests.
